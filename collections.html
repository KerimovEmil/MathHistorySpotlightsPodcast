The Trinity

Mathematical Partnership

Experience the legendary collaboration between G.H. Hardy and J.E. Littlewood, and their discovery of the self-taught genius Srinivasa Ramanujan. This group represents one of the most productive and poetic eras in Cambridge mathematics.

The Bernoulli Dynasty

Family Feuds

The Bernoullis were a family of Swiss mathematicians whose brilliance was matched only by their fierce internal rivalries. From the foundations of calculus to the principles of fluid dynamics, they redefined what a scientific family could achieve.

The Great Rivalries

Scientific Revolution

Behind the most profound discoveries in science and mathematics lay fierce intellectual battles. From the dispute over the invention of calculus to the nature of gravity, discover the intense rivalries between these giants of the 17th century.

Foundations of Computing

Digital Revolution

Long before the first silicon chip, these visionaries laid the intellectual groundwork for the modern computer. From mechanical engines to Boolean logic, information theory, and game theory, discover how the digital age was born across two centuries.

Probability Begins

Risk & Logic

From gambling problems to classical probability theory. These thinkers transformed our understanding of chance, risk, and uncertainty, moving mathematics from deterministic proofs to statistical models.

French Analysis Trio

Applied Analysis

PDEs, heat, orthogonal functions, and applied analysis. During a golden age of French mathematics, these three redefined how we use math to describe the physical world.

Women in Early Mathematics

Pioneering Spirits

Breaking through the barriers of their time, these women made fundamental contributions to theoretical and applied mathematics, inspiring generations of mathematicians to follow.

The Italian Cubic Wars

Renaissance Drama

One of the most dramatic episodes in mathematical history. Stolen proofs, public debates, and accusations of poisoning surrounded the race to solve the cubic equation in 16th-century Italy. Listen to these four stories together to follow the full, scandalous saga.

The Abel–Galois Generation

Tragic Geniuses

Two of the greatest mathematical minds of the 19th century, both dead before 30. Abel and Galois independently revolutionized algebra and group theory while struggling for recognition. Liouville was the man who finally brought Galois's work to the world.

The Göttingen School

Abstract Algebra & Modern Math

From roughly 1870 to 1933, the University of Göttingen was the world center of mathematics. These four figures shaped modern abstract mathematics — from the 23 Hilbert problems to the birth of abstract algebra — before the school was tragically dismantled by the Nazis.

The Electromagnetic Theorists

Physics-Math Bridge

Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism with mathematics — and then these three took that torch and ran with it. Kelvin, Heaviside, and Hertz each extended and experimentally confirmed electromagnetic theory, reshaping how we understand waves, fields, and energy.

Game Theory & Information Age

20th Century Icons

Three towering figures who defined how the 20th century thinks about strategy, computation, and information. Von Neumann invented game theory; Shannon invented information theory; Nash extended both into new and unexpected territory.

The Precalculus Pioneers

Medieval & Renaissance

Centuries before Newton and Leibniz, mathematicians across the Islamic world, South Asia, and Europe were quietly building the foundations that calculus would rest upon — infinite series, trigonometry, positional arithmetic, and algebraic notation.
